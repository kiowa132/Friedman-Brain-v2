# Sell vs. Rent Analysis — Standing Procedure

When a client asks "should I sell or rent out my home?", produce a
**sell vs. rent vs. HELOC** comparison broken down from multiple angles.

Worked example (first run, Aug 2026): 8303 Bellona Ave, Towson —
`Desktop/8303 Bellona Ave/Sell-vs-Rent-Analysis-8303-Bellona.md` and the
client Gamma report https://sell-vs-rent-8303-bellon-zje9ww3.gamma.site/

## Inputs to collect from the client
- Market/list value (build a CMA) + a seller net-proceeds worksheet
- Mortgage payoff, **monthly P&I** — get the statement; whether the
  payment is PITI or P&I-only is the single biggest swing factor
- Rate, term; any open **HELOC / second lien**
- Primary residence? Move-in date? Filing status (single vs. MFJ)?
- Capital improvements since purchase + purchase closing costs (cost basis)
- Condition — rent-ready vs. list-ready cost
- Client's plan for the proceeds, risk tolerance, landlord appetite

## The four aspects to break down
1. **Cash today** — net sale proceeds vs. $0 + monthly rental cash flow
2. **Annual return** — proceeds invested (~5%) vs. rental cash flow +
   principal paydown + appreciation (model **3%/yr**, conservative)
3. **Risk & effort** — vacancy, tenant, CapEx on an older home, MD/county
   compliance, leverage — vs. a clean one-time exit
4. **Taxes** — the Section 121 exclusion below is usually the deciding
   factor and most owners have never heard of it

## Section 121 primary-residence exclusion — the key point
- Own + live in as primary home **2 of the last 5 years** → exclude up to
  **$250K gain (single) / $500K (married filing jointly)**, federal and MD.
- Exclusion is preserved if the home sells within **~3 years** of moving
  out (still passes 2-of-5). Rent **3+ years → lose it entirely**; the
  whole gain plus all appreciation becomes taxable (~15-20% fed + 5.75%
  MD + local).
- Depreciation taken while renting (land-adjusted basis ÷ 27.5) is
  **recaptured at up to 25%** on sale regardless of the exclusion.
- Bottom line: selling now, or renting on a defined ≤3-year clock,
  protects a tax-free gain that a long hold destroys.

## Rental expense model (annual; older MD single-family, full management)
- Vacancy **6%** of rent · Management **9%** of rent · Leasing fee ~75% of
  one month, amortized over a ~2-yr tenancy
- Repairs/maintenance ~**1.5% of value** (older home) or ~8% of rent
- CapEx reserve ~**15% of rent** for a pre-1980 house + a $5-10K emergency
  reserve
- Landlord insurance (DP-3) ~$1,500-1,800; roughly +$400-700 vs. an owner
  policy
- Property tax — note the owner-occupied Homestead cap unwinds once it's a
  rental, so taxes drift up
- **Baltimore County:** rental license + licensed inspection, renewed
  every 3 years
- **Pre-1978 homes:** Maryland MDE lead rental registration + risk
  reduction / inspection
- A ~75-80% expense ratio is normal for an older home fully managed;
  ~45-55% self-managed and in good repair
- Show cash flow at **two rent levels** (market + a stretch/optimistic)
  × **two management modes** (full management + self-managed)

## Also cover
- **HELOC / cash-out** as a third path: keep the home, borrow ~80-85%
  CLTV tax-free (it's a loan) — but it adds a variable-rate payment,
  doesn't answer the landlord question, and doesn't stop the 3-year
  exclusion clock if he later rents.
- **5-year side-by-side:** sell + invest vs. rent 5 yrs then sell vs.
  rent ~3 yrs then sell (keeps the exclusion).
- Disclaimer on every version: not tax/legal/investment advice; client
  confirms with a CPA.

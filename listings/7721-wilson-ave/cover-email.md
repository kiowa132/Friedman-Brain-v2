**First, the good news. Your house did not fail. The marketing did.**

You already did the hard part. The home is fully renovated, the big systems are handled, and it is vacant and ready to show. The Parkville market is fine right now: homes that are priced and presented right are going under contract in about three and a half weeks at close to full price. A move-in ready home with a finished lower level should be one of the faster sales on the block. Every reason it stalled is fixable.

**Here is what I found, in plain terms.**

**1. The square footage on the MLS was wrong, and that was the biggest single problem.** Listings show above-grade square footage, and that is what buyers filter on when they search. 7721 Wilson was entered at about 1,287 square feet. Zillow, Redfin, and the older sale records all show it at roughly 1,700. That 400 square foot gap did two things:

  - It kept the listing out of a large share of buyer searches. Many buyers set a minimum size, usually 1,400 or 1,500 square feet and up, and your home was filtered out before anyone saw a photo.
  - It made the price look high. At the listed size, $324,900 works out to $252 a square foot, which reads as expensive. At the real size it is about $191 a square foot, which is right in line with the market. Same house, same price, completely different impression. And that is before the finished lower level is even counted.

**2. The sequence was backwards.** The order matters more than any single tactic. The first run went live, then sat, then the photos were re-ordered, then AI staging was added several weeks in, then the price was cut, then cut again, then the listing was pulled. That is executing first and planning afterward, in public, while buyers watch. The photos themselves were about as good as they could be. The problem was that staging, pricing, and the launch schedule are decisions you lock in before the home is ever visible, because the first week is where most offers come from and you cannot re-run it.

**3. The staging was basic, and it was late.** A few photos got one AI furniture look, added after the listing had already gone quiet. One style, applied late, to buyers who had stopped watching. That does not build any attachment, and attachment is what produces an offer. Buyers decide with emotion and then justify it with logic. The job of the online presentation is to get a buyer to spend enough time with the home to picture their family in it.

**The fix is a clean relaunch, planned first and then executed once.**

  - Get the home professionally measured and correct the square footage on the MLS, so it lands in the searches it was locked out of and the price-per-foot story flips from expensive to smart buy.
  - Zillow Showcase, which is a different thing from basic AI staging. It includes a full 3D Matterport tour a buyer can walk through from their couch, an interactive floor plan so the main-level primary and the finished lower level are obvious, and interactive virtual staging in multiple design styles, so a buyer who likes contemporary sees it contemporary and one who likes traditional or modern farmhouse flips it to that. Each buyer sees the home the way they would actually live in it. Showcase listings also sit above standard listings in search, and they are 20% more likely to get an accepted offer within two weeks.
  - Professional photos and video, Google and social ads from day one, a single-property website, and full syndication.
  - A launch built around the buyer's week. We go Coming Soon on a Thursday, which is when buyers start planning their weekend, and hold it there for a full week to bank saves and tour requests and confirm the price is right. We go active the following Thursday, so every buyer who saved it plus every new buyer gets it fresh right before their weekend tours. Redfin's data shows homes that go active on Thursday go under contract about five days faster. Then a public open house and a brokers open that first Saturday and Sunday, when the listing is newest and traffic peaks, so buyers are in the house at the same time and see the competition. Offers come in early the next week.

**It comes back as a new listing.** A new MLS number resets the days-on-market count to zero, and with new photos, a new floor plan, the corrected square footage, and Showcase, it reads as a brand new listing to every buyer. Fall is also a strong selling season, so the serious, ready buyers are out right now.

**On price**, once the presentation is fixed you have two good options:

  1. **$319,000**, priced to be the clear best value in the area, to pull a wave of showings and offers the first weekend, with a real shot at bidding back toward $330,000 or higher.
  2. **$324,900 to $329,900**, priced right and held firm, with one decisive move only if it is genuinely slow after about three weeks. No ladder of cuts.

First, the basement. Sorry you are dealing with that on top of everything else. Here is how I would handle it:

1. Get it dried out now. Call a water mitigation company today or tomorrow and get extraction, dehumidifiers, and air movers running. You have about 48 hours before wet drywall and framing start growing mold, so speed matters more than anything else right now.

2. Document everything before you touch it. Photos and video of the water line on the walls, the flooring, and anything damaged. That is for your insurance claim and for the disclosure later.

3. Find the actual cause. Groundwater coming up, a plumbing failure, or surface water coming in through the walk-up. The walk-up exit is a common entry point. Whatever it is, the fix is not just new drywall, it is solving the water: a sump pump, a regrade, a drain at the walk-up, whatever the source calls for.

4. Fix the cause, then the finishes. Once it is dry and the source is handled, replace only what needs replacing. Wet drywall gets cut back to dry material, carpet usually goes, luxury vinyl plank can sometimes be saved. Keep every receipt.

5. Disclosure. Maryland requires you to disclose known water intrusion, so the goal is a clean story: flooded on this date, cause was X, professionally remediated, and here is the sump pump or drain we added so it does not happen again. Handled that way it is a footnote, not a red flag. We do not list until it is dry, fixed, and documented.

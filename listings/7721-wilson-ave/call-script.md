# Call Script — Nikki Fern, 7721 Wilson Ave

Phone, today 4:30 PM. She reached out, she has the breakdown PDF and email.
Goal of this call: give her confidence back, show her you know exactly what
went wrong and how to fix it, and book the in-person at the house.

Rhythm: Ask, say one thing back, then a question that opens the next step.
Confident and matter-of-fact. No "no pressure." "Fill out the listing
paperwork," not "sign the agreement."

---

## 1. OPEN

**Ask.** "Hey Nikki, Kyle Friedman. Good to finally talk. Did you get a chance
to look at the breakdown I sent over?"

**Then.** "No worries if not, we'll walk through it together, that's what this
call is for."

**Lead in.** "Before I give you my take, I want to hear yours. What's your gut
telling you about how the listing went?"

---

## 2. HER SIDE FIRST

**Ask.** "What did your agent tell you was going on? ... When you had no
showings that first week, what did they change? ... How are you feeling about it
now that it's come off the market?"

**Then.** Let her talk. Do not interrupt. When she's done: "Okay. That lines up
with what I found."

**Lead in.** "Tell me about the house itself. It's fully renovated and vacant,
right?"

---

## 3. THE HOUSE

**Ask.** "Kitchen's brand new, the lower level's finished with the full bath and
the walk-up, main-level primary, new roof, boiler, mini-splits, sewer line. Am I
missing anything? ... Anything else you updated? ... You're fully moved out to
Hampstead?"

**Then.** "Good. So the house is done. That's important, because it means this
isn't a condition problem, and it isn't a Parkville problem. Homes like yours in
21234 are going under contract in about three weeks right now."

**Lead in.** "How soon do you need this sold, now that you've moved?"

---

## 4. HER SITUATION

**Ask.** "Is there a hard deadline, or is it more that you just want it done and
off your plate? ... Do you still have a mortgage on it, and roughly what's the
balance? ... And are you fully clear of the agreement with your last agent?"

**Then.** "Okay. I'll build you a net sheet so you see your exact walk-away
number at each price." **If she's not clear on the agreement status, see the
separate "Canceling with Mari" script for exactly how to walk her through
it.**

**Lead in.** "Let me walk you through what actually went wrong. Three things, and
none of them are the house or you."

---

## 5. WHAT WENT WRONG

**Say it, tight.**
"One. The square footage on the MLS was wrong. It was entered around 1,287
square feet. Zillow, Redfin, and the old records all show it closer to 1,700.
That kept your listing out of every buyer search that filters for 1,400 or 1,500
and up, and it made the price look high. At 1,287 feet, $324,900 reads as $252 a
foot, expensive. At the real size it's about $191 a foot, right in line with the
market. Same house, same price, opposite impression.

Two. The launch. The first week is where most of your buyer traffic comes, and
photos and staging got patched in during week two, after that window was
already gone.

Three. Two price cuts in twenty-five days. When buyers see a price drop twice in
three weeks, they stop making offers and wait for the next cut. The listing
stalled and came off."

**Lead in.** "Does that match what you saw from your side?"

---

## 6. THE FIX

**Say it.**
"The fix is a clean relaunch, and we do it right now, into the fall market.
New MLS number, which resets the days-on-market to zero. We get the home
professionally measured so the square footage is right and it lands in the
searches it belongs in. Interactive floor plan so the main-level primary and the
finished lower level actually show. Zillow Showcase, which is a real 3D tour and
interactive staging in different design styles, so each buyer sees it the way
they'd live in it. Professional photos and video. Paid Google and social ads
from day one.

Then the launch is timed to how buyers actually shop. Coming Soon on a Thursday,
we hold it a week to build a waiting list, go active the next Thursday right
before the weekend, open house and brokers open that Saturday and Sunday. Offers
come in the week after. One price, set right, and we hold it. No cut ladder."

**Lead in.** "Any of that raise a question for you?"

---

## 7. PRICE

**Ask.** "What were you hoping to get for it? ... Where did that number come
from?"

**Then.** "Here's my read. With the square footage fixed and the listing
presented right, you've got two ways to go. One is around $319,000, priced to be
the clear best value in the pocket, which pulls a wave of showings and can bid
back up toward $330,000 or more. The other is $324,900 to $329,900, priced right
and held firm. Two homes the same finished size as yours are under contract
right now at $349,900 and $359,000, so there's room. Honestly, it should have
sold the first time around $329,900."

**Lead in.** "The real number, with your net sheet, I give you when I'm standing
in the house. That's about thirty minutes. Does tomorrow work, or is later this
week better?"

---

## 8. IF SHE PUSHES ON TIMING

"October first is a strong time to list. Fall is the second-best selling season
of the year, right behind spring. Spring has more buyers, but it also has three
or four times the listings, so you're one of a crowd. The buyers out right now
are the serious ones, pre-approved and wanting to be settled before the
holidays, and there's far less competition. Waiting for spring means sitting
through the slowest months and then launching into that flood. The new MLS
number resets your days on market now, so there's nothing to wait for."

---

## 9. BOOK + COMMITMENT

**Then.** "When I come out I'll bring the sold comps, your net sheet at each
price, and the full plan and calendar. We measure the house, lock the number,
and set the Coming Soon and launch dates."

**Lead in.** "If the plan and the number make sense when we sit down, are you
ready to fill out the listing paperwork and get it moving, or are you still
deciding whether to sell at all?"

---

## 10. CONFIRM

**Ask.** "Who else is part of this decision, anyone who should be there? ...
We're meeting at the house? ... Best cell for you?"

**Then.** "It's about thirty minutes. I'll text you a confirmation with what to
look at first."

**Close.** "Talk [day]."

---

## OBJECTIONS

**"I feel bad leaving my agent."**
"I get that. You gave her a full run, and the listing came off the market. You
reached out to me for a straight answer, and this is your house and your money.
Nobody's asking you to be unkind, just to run it right this time."

**"My agent said the market's just slow."**
"21234 is clearing homes in about three weeks at basically full price. A
well-run listing here does not sit. The market did its part."

**"Can't you just tell me the price now?"**
"I can give you a range on the phone, and I just did. The number I'd actually
list at, with your net sheet, I owe you in person. It'll be off by fifteen or
twenty grand if I guess it blind."

**"Should I wait and relist in the spring?"**
"Waiting costs you twice. You sit through November through January, the slowest
stretch, and then you launch into every other seller doing the same thing. New
MLS number resets your days on market now. We sell while the others wait."

**"Let me think about it / talk to my husband."**
"Makes sense, you know him better than I do. What do you think he'll say? Let's
get both of you in the room when I come out so I'm not answering through you."

**The pass (only if everything's soft):**
"Honestly, if you're not sure you're selling, I'd rather you take your time than
rush it. I'll send you the sold data for your street. When you know your
timeline, call me."

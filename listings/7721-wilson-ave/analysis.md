## THE #1 FINDING: the square footage was listed wrong

- Bright MLS one-line for 7721 Wilson: **1,287 sf above grade / 2,145 sf total
  finished.** Every other source (Zillow, Redfin, tax blends) shows ~1,655 to
  1,722. The finished lower level (rec room + full bath) is real, permitted-look
  space that was not carried into the number buyers search on.
- **What that did:**
  1. **Search invisibility.** Buyers who filter "1,400+ sf" or "1,500+ sf" never
     saw it. A renovated 3/2 cape with a finished lower level should read as a
     ~2,100 sf home. It was listed as a ~1,300 sf home.
  2. **Broken price-per-foot.** $324,900 / 1,287 = **$252/sf**, which reads
     "expensive" against every comp. $324,900 / 2,145 = **$151/sf**, which reads
     "great value." Same house, same price, opposite impression.
- **Fix:** get it measured properly (Cape Cod half-stories are chronically
  under-counted on 60-year-old assessor cards), enter accurate above-grade with a
  floor-plan source, and market the full ~2,100+ finished square feet everywhere,
  including the fields buyers filter on. An interactive floor plan (comes with
  Zillow Showcase) makes the space legible.

## Timing: relaunch now, do not wait (per Kyle, Sept 10)

Nikki does not want to wait months for days-on-market to reset, and she should
not have to.

- **Bright MLS:** DOM and CDOM reset to zero for a **new listing (new MLS
  number)** entered **61 days** after the prior listing was withdrawn (not 90).
  Withdrawn Sept 6, so a full Bright CDOM wipe needs a new MLS number on or
  after ~Nov 6. Flipping the same withdrawn MLS number back to Active does not
  reset anything.
- **Zillow:** "days on Zillow" fully resets after ~90 days off Zillow; sooner
  than that it keeps the old count and flags "relisted."
- **What matters:** a new MLS number now resets the **public DOM to zero**
  immediately, which is the number buyers and most portal displays lead with.
  CDOM (~44 and climbing) is visible to agents who open the MLS detail, but the
  first run had only a handful of showings before it came off, so there is
  almost no story to carry. The presentation reset (new photos, floor plan,
  corrected sqft, Showcase, new price and remarks) is what actually changes
  buyer perception.
- **The fall case for moving now:** September and October are the year's second
  selling window. Fall buyers are more serious, pre-approved, and motivated to
  close before year-end. Waiting to December/January means launching into the
  slowest weeks (winter ~49 DOM vs spring ~33) and then competing with the
  spring flood of new inventory. Do not claim "fall is literally as hot as
  spring" as fact (data says spring is the peak); frame it as a strong second
  window with real advantages (serious buyers, less casual competition).

## The new 4-step framework (replaces prep / show / feedback / negotiate)

**How a home actually sells. A listing stalls when it loses one of these.**

1. **Get Found.** Show up in the searches buyers are running: accurate and full
   square footage, the right price band, complete listing data, syndication
   everywhere. *7721 Wilson lost this one badly (listed at 1,287 sf).*
2. **Get the Click.** The first photo, the price, and the headline earn the tap.
   Zillow Showcase places it above standard listings in the results.
   *7721 Wilson: reactive photo shuffle in week two, no Showcase, stale by then.*
3. **Get the Showing.** The online tour makes a buyer need to see it in person:
   interactive floor plan, 3D tour, virtual staging, video. *7721 Wilson: no
   floor plan, so the main-level primary and finished lower level did not read.*
4. **Get the Offer.** Read showing feedback, hold one correct price with a plan,
   negotiate. *7721 Wilson: two cuts in 25 days trained buyers to wait, then it
   was withdrawn.*

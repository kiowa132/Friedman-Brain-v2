**Answer:** the buyer does not automatically get equity. The equity comes from the price they pay and from keeping the renovation disciplined, since the ceiling is roughly $425,000 to $445,000. At list price with a full gut renovation they are paying near the top of the market once the work is finished. At a **$345,000 to $350,000** purchase and a **$50,000 to $60,000** scope, there is a real but modest cushion of about $20,000 to $30,000. This is a value-add play on the lot and the lower level, not a home-run flip.

**How to protect the equity:**
1. Negotiate the price down using the outdated finishes (opening $335,000 to $342,000).
2. Keep the renovation to what buyers in this price band pay for: kitchen, baths, flooring, paint, lighting. Don't build to luxury standards past the $445,000 ceiling.
3. Get contractor bids and an inspection before removing contingencies: roof, HVAC, windows, sewer, electrical panel, and moisture in the walkout.
4. The lower level is the highest use of money: it already has a full bath and walkout, so finishing it out adds space the comps with garages and smaller lots lack.
5. Consider adding a garage or covered parking if permitted: no garage is the biggest gap against Nelson Rd and the Woodglen and Church Hill comps.

**Subject:** Your Bethesda condo, why it did not sell and how I would sell it

Hi Wendy,

Good talking with you. As promised, I went through everything on 8302 Woodmont
#203 and put together a straight breakdown: why the last listing did not sell,
exactly what I would do differently, and a net proceeds estimate at three sale
prices. It is all attached.

First, why it did not sell. It was a pricing and launch problem, not a house
problem. The listing went on at $775,000 in February and came down in eight
separate price cuts to $685,000 over six months before it expired. When buyers
watch a price get cut that many times, they stop making offers and wait for the
next one, and after roughly 180 days the listing goes stale and disappears from
their searches. The unit itself is excellent: built 2017, corner exposure,
private balcony, two deeded garage spaces, a full service building. None of that
was the issue. The one unit you compete with directly, #305 in your building, has
been for sale most of the year and is still sitting at $730,000. Recent 2 bedroom
sales in downtown Bethesda support a clean relaunch around $671,000 to $697,000.

Now, how I would actually sell it. This is a full relaunch, not a price change on
the old listing, and the marketing is where most of the difference is.

We start with a complete reset. For Bright MLS and Zillow to zero out the days on
market count and drop the old price history, the unit has to be off the market
for 90 days. Yours came off August 21, so the slate is clean around the middle to
end of November. We use that window to build everything below, then launch fresh.

The photography gets redone properly. The current photos are dim and soft and do
not show how much light the corner unit pulls in. New shoot with proper lighting
and a wide angle lens, plus a twilight exterior of the building.

Buyers then get a full Matterport 3D tour so they can walk the entire unit online
before they ever schedule a showing, an interactive floor plan, and a video
walkthrough.

Your unit goes on Zillow Showcase. This is Zillow's premium listing placement,
only available through a limited set of agents. It gives the unit an elevated
page that sits above standard listings in search, with the 3D tour and
interactive floor plan built in, and Zillow's data shows Showcase listings get
far more views, saves, and shares. Overview:
https://www.zillow.com/premier-agent/showcase/ &nbsp; Quick look:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=32FY8L9_NsE

Around that, we run a dedicated single property website for the unit, syndication
to Zillow, Redfin, and realtor.com, and a Coming Soon runway of about two weeks
before we go live to build a waiting list of interested buyers so we launch with
momentum instead of from zero.

On the agent side, we send an email blast to every brokerage in the area, host a
brokers open so local agents tour it in person, and make direct calls to agents
who have active 2 bedroom buyers right now. We also run paid digital and social
across the DC and Maryland buyer market and mail a print piece to the building
and the surrounding blocks.

Throughout, you get a weekly call on showing feedback, traffic, and pricing, and
showing feedback back to you within 48 hours every time.

Two ways to price the relaunch. Option A is $690,000, priced to sell, $40,000
under the competing unit, with an offer likely inside 30 days. Option B is more
aggressive: relaunch at $675,000, a number buyers and agents cannot argue with,
to pull a wave of showings the first weekend and a real shot at competing offers
that bid the price back toward or past $690,000. I would want to talk through
which one fits your situation.

One thing I want to be upfront about: your loan balance sits close to today's
value, so the net is thin at every price. That is why the whole plan is built
around speed. The marketing above is designed to sell this in weeks, not to carry
it for another six months.

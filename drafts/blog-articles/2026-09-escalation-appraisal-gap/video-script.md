# Video Script — Escalation Clauses & Appraisal Gaps

Format: 15-20 sec vertical (9:16). Story/hook-driven, not Kyle reciting
data. One short Kyle line at the end; everything else is b-roll + on-screen
text so it can be cut fast. Numbers match the article ($525K offer →
escalates to $548K → appraises at $525K → ~$23K gap).

## Post caption / hook line
"She won the bidding war and lost $23,000 the same afternoon. Here's the
clause that did both."

## Script

| Time | Visual (edit direction) | Audio / On-screen text |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3s | Cold open. Fast push-in on a "SOLD" rider snapping onto a For Sale sign, bright morning light. Hard cut on the beat. | **On-screen (big):** "Won the house. Lost $23,000. Same afternoon." |
| 3-6s | Split screen or quick A/B cuts: left = "ESCALATION CLAUSE", right = "APPRAISAL GAP". Clean kinetic type on cream background, brand teal/gold. | **On-screen:** "Two clauses. One wins the house. One can cost you." |
| 6-10s | Number counter ticks up fast: $525,000 → $548,000, a small gold ▲, "YOU WIN" stamp. | **VO (Kyle, calm):** "The escalation clause won the bidding war at five-forty-eight." |
| 10-14s | Hard cut. An "APPRAISED" stamp drops lower on screen at $525,000. Red wedge animates in between the two numbers: "$23,000". | **VO (Kyle):** "Three weeks later it appraised for five-twenty-five. She'd already promised to cover the gap — in cash." |
| 14-17s | Cut to two clean lines building on screen over blurred desk b-roll. | **On-screen:** "Know your two numbers first —  1) your cap   2) your gap cash" |
| 17-20s | Kyle, brief, direct to camera (chest-up), then cut to end card. | **VO (Kyle):** "Let's set both before you write the offer, not after."  **End card:** "The Friedman Team · Numbers Over Guesswork" |

## No-face alt (shoot even faster)
Drop the two Kyle VO lines; run them as on-screen text over b-roll and let
the counter/stamp animations carry it. Keep the final line as text only:
"Set both numbers before you write the offer. — The Friedman Team."

## B-roll shot list
- For Sale sign, "SOLD" rider snapping on (or stock)
- Hand setting a pen down on a contract (no signing-as-triumph — keep it neutral)
- Laptop/calculator with a number field, blurred
- Kyle chest-up, plain wall, one take of each VO line

# Escalation Clauses and Appraisal Gaps: What Maryland Homebuyers Need to Know Before Making an Offer

In a competitive Maryland real estate market, two pieces of contract
language show up in nearly every serious offer: the escalation clause and
the appraisal gap. Both can help you win a bidding war. Both can also cost
you far more than you planned if you don't fully understand how they
work. Here's what each one actually does, how they interact, and what to
weigh before you write your next offer.

## What Is an Escalation Clause?

An escalation clause is language added to a purchase offer that
automatically increases your bid above any competing offer, by a set
increment, up to a maximum price you specify in advance.

A typical escalation clause has three components:

- **Proof of a competing offer** — the seller must show evidence of
  another bona fide offer before the clause activates
- **The escalation increment** — the fixed amount your offer increases
  above the competing bid (for example, $2,000 above the next-highest
  offer)
- **The price cap** — the absolute ceiling you're willing to pay,
  regardless of how high competing offers go

If two buyers both submit escalation clauses on the same property, both
trigger, and the price climbs automatically until one buyer's cap is
reached.

### The Risk With Escalation Clauses

The main downside: once a seller sees your price cap, they've effectively
seen your maximum willingness to pay. Some real estate agents structure
escalation clauses to disclose only the current winning bid amount at
each step, keeping the buyer's true ceiling private until it's actually
needed. This is worth discussing directly with your agent before you
submit an offer.

## What Is an Appraisal Gap?

An appraisal gap is the difference between your agreed purchase price and
the value a lender's appraiser assigns to the home. Because most lenders
will only finance up to the appraised value, any gap between your offer
and the appraisal becomes cash you must bring to the table yourself if
you still want the purchase to close at the original price.

Appraisals are based on recent comparable sales. In a fast-moving,
competitive market, those comps can lag behind what buyers are actually
willing to pay — which is exactly how homes sell well above asking price
and still leave buyers responsible for a significant appraisal-gap
payment.

## How Escalation Clauses and Appraisal Gaps Work Together

To remain competitive, many buyers now pair an escalation clause with an
upfront commitment to cover some or all of a potential appraisal gap in
cash — often before they know exactly how large that gap will turn out to
be.

This is where buyers can get into real trouble: agreeing in advance to
cover an unknown-sized gap is a materially different commitment than
simply agreeing to a purchase price. Before making that promise, know
precisely how much additional cash you can bring beyond your planned down
payment if the appraisal comes in below your offer.

## What to Do Before You Write Your Offer

- Determine your true price ceiling in advance, before you're reacting to
  competing offers in real time
- Calculate exactly how much cash you could bring to cover an appraisal
  gap, separate from your down payment
- Ask your agent whether your escalation clause needs to reveal your full
  price cap, or only the current leading bid
- Work with an agent who has recent, direct experience writing escalation
  and appraisal-gap language for buyers in your specific local market

## The Bottom Line

Escalation clauses and appraisal gap coverage are powerful tools for
winning a competitive offer — but only when you understand your real
limits before you use them. Know your numbers first, then decide how
aggressively to compete.

## Post — long version (buyer angle, ~1,320 chars)

Won the bidding war, then lost $23,000 the same month. One line in the contract did both.

If you're buying in Carroll, Baltimore, Howard, or Frederick County right now, two pieces of contract language decide more than almost anything else: the escalation clause and appraisal gap coverage. Most buyers have heard the terms. Few know what they actually commit you to.

An escalation clause automatically raises your offer above competing bids, in set steps, up to a maximum you choose. In Maryland it's a standard Realtors addendum submitted with your original offer, and the seller must show proof of a real competing offer before it triggers.

An appraisal gap is separate — the difference between your price and the lender's appraised value. Because lenders only finance up to that value, the gap is cash you bring to closing, on top of your down payment. Appraisal gap coverage is your promise to cover some or all of it.

Here's where it gets expensive: buyers pair the two to win — escalating the price up while promising to cover a gap they can't size yet. If the price outruns the appraisal, you owe the difference in cash, after you've already beaten every other buyer.

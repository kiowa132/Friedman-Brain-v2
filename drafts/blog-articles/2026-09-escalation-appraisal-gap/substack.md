# The Clause That Wins Bidding Wars — And the One That Can Blow Up Your Deal

You've found the house. So has everyone else. Two pieces of contract
language decide more offers than almost anything: the escalation clause
and the appraisal gap. Get either one wrong and it can cost you the house
— or a lot more cash than you meant to spend.

**Escalation clauses**, in short: your offer automatically climbs above
any competing bid, up to a cap you set. If the seller can prove another
real offer exists, it triggers — otherwise it stays dormant. The catch?
Whoever sees your cap number has seen your whole hand. Ask your agent
whether the clause needs to reveal your ceiling, or just the current top
bid.

**Appraisal gaps** are the space between your offer and what the lender's
appraiser actually says the home is worth. Since lenders only finance up
to that appraised value, any gap becomes cash you owe out of pocket if
you still want the deal to close at your price. In a hot market, comps
lag reality — that's exactly how a home sells well over asking and leaves
the buyer covering tens of thousands extra.

Buyers increasingly promise upfront to cover part or all of that gap —
before they even know how big it'll be. That's the real risk: you're
committing to a number you can't yet see.

**Before you write your next offer:** know your true price ceiling before
you're under pressure, know exactly how much cash you can bring beyond
your down payment, and talk to someone who's actually written this
language for buyers in this market — not a generic template.

TITLE: Prices Held. The Clock Didn't. | Maryland Weekly Market Report, Week of September 14-20, 2026

SUBTITLE: Mortgage rates jumped 19 basis points and Maryland prices barely moved. The number that did move is the one sellers are sleeping on.

BYLINE: Kyle Friedman, Sep 21, 2026

---

Denise and Walt listed their Frederick County house on Monday, September 14th. Their agent had run the comps and suggested $525,000. Walt wanted $549,900. "We can always come down," he said, and Denise, who had sold a house once before, agreed to try it.

By Thursday there had been eleven showings and no offers. That same day, Freddie Mac announced the 30-year mortgage rate had jumped to 6.95%, up 19 basis points in a single week and the fourth increase in a row. On a $400,000 loan, that is about $51 more a month than the week before. Every buyer who had toured their house was quietly running that math.

Here is what Denise and Walt could not see from their kitchen table. Across Maryland, the typical home that closed this week had taken 25 days to go under contract, up from 20 the week before. The price side of the market barely flinched: the median sold price rose 1.1% to $449,900. But buyers took a little more off the original asking price, paying 98.4% of it, down from 99.6%. Price held. Time and negotiating room were what gave.

Their agent saw it in the data, and on Friday morning made the call. Denise and Walt trusted the numbers and dropped to $529,900, still well above the first comps. Two showings came in that weekend. By Sunday night, they had one offer in hand, and a second buyer was coming back for another look on Monday.

They are not done yet, but they are in a very different place than they were on Thursday. A house is only new once, and the sellers who win this market are the ones who read the numbers and move while the window is open.

## This Week's Price Extremes

**Highest-priced new listing:** $9,500,000, in Anne Arundel County. **Lowest-priced new listing:** $10,000, in Allegany County and Baltimore City. The highest closed sale of the week was $5,437,500 in Anne Arundel County, and the lowest was $11,000 in Baltimore City.

Slowest county: Garrett County, a 102-day average on market on 10 closings.

## The Friedman Market Momentum Index: 44/100, Balanced Market, Cooling

Down four points from last week. Price and supply held up (Seller Strength 55, Demand 54), but the clock and the rate moved the wrong way: Market Speed slipped to 42 and Rate Environment fell to 24. Nothing broke. The market cooled at the edges.

## Deep Dive: The Number That Explains This Week

**1.6 points** is the gap between what sellers first asked and what they finally got for the typical Maryland home that closed this week: 98.4% of original list price, versus 100.0% of final list price. Last week that gap was 0.4 points. It quadrupled in seven days.

Sellers did not lose ground at the closing table. What changed is how many had to get there by cutting. On a $450,000 home, 1.6 points is about $7,200, compared with about $1,800 last week. And the other half of the cost is time: 25 days to contract instead of 20. The cheapest price cut is the one you never have to make.

## One Thing I'd Do This Week

**Buying?** Lock your rate. It rose 19 basis points this week and has now risen four weeks running. The upside is more room to negotiate: homes sold at 98.4% of original list, and there are 2,269 new listings to choose from. Spend that leverage on price and terms, not on waiting for a better rate.

**Selling?** Price for week one. The typical home took 25 days to go under contract, five more than last week, and homes priced right on day one still sold at 100.0% of final list price. The ones that launch high pay for it in time.

**Investing?** Look where the clock is slowest and pricing is softest. Garrett (102 days) and Caroline (98 days) are moving slowest, and Worcester sold at 95.8% of original list. That is where motivated sellers live right now.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Apple Cider Pork Chops

Maryland apple season is in full swing, from the orchards in Carroll and Frederick counties to the farm stands along the Shore. A one-skillet dinner that leans on exactly what is in season, about 35 minutes start to finish.

**Serves 4**

1. Pat 4 bone-in pork chops (about 1 inch thick) dry and season with 1 teaspoon salt and 1/2 teaspoon pepper. Sear in 2 tablespoons olive oil over medium-high heat, about 4 minutes per side, then move to a plate.
2. Lower the heat to medium. Cook 1 sliced yellow onion for 4 minutes, add 2 cored, sliced firm apples (Honeycrisp or Stayman Winesap) and cook 3 minutes more.
3. Pour in 1 cup unfiltered apple cider, 1 tablespoon Dijon, 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar, and 4 sprigs thyme. Scrape up the browned bits and simmer 3 minutes.
4. Nestle the chops back in, cover, and cook on low 8 to 10 minutes, until the center reaches 145 degrees F. Rest 3 minutes.
5. Stir in 1 tablespoon butter and spoon the apples, onions, and sauce over the chops.

Serve with mashed potatoes or roasted sweet potatoes and a bitter green salad to balance the sweetness.

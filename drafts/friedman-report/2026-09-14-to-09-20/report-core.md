## The Story of the Week

Denise and Walt listed their Frederick County house on Monday, September 14th.

Their agent had run the comps and suggested $525,000. Walt wanted $549,900.
"We can always come down," he said, and Denise, who had sold a house once
before, agreed to try it. Prices were holding. Nothing in the headlines said
otherwise.

By Thursday there had been eleven showings and no offers. That same day,
Freddie Mac announced the 30-year mortgage rate had jumped to 6.95%, up 19
basis points in a single week and the fourth increase in a row. On a
$400,000 loan, that is about $51 more a month than the week before, and $77
more than three weeks ago. Every buyer who had toured their house was quietly
running that math.

Here is what Denise and Walt could not see from their kitchen table. Across
Maryland, the typical home that closed this week had taken 25 days to go
under contract, up from 20 the week before. The price side of the market
barely flinched: the median sold price rose 1.1% to $449,900. But buyers
took a little more off the original asking price, paying 98.4% of it, down
from 99.6%. Price held. Time and negotiating room were what gave.

Their agent saw it in the data, and on Friday morning made the call. Denise
and Walt trusted the numbers and dropped to $529,900, still well above the
first comps. Two showings came in that weekend. By Sunday night, they had one
offer in hand, and a second buyer was coming back for another look on Monday.

They are not done yet, but they are in a very different place than they were
on Thursday. The lesson is simple: a house is only new once, and the sellers
who win this market are the ones who read the numbers and move while the
window is open.

## What the Numbers Really Mean

**The clock moved more than the price.** The median time to contract for homes
that closed this week rose to 25 days from 20, a five-day jump in one week
and the largest move this report has tracked since it began measuring it
this way in late August. The median sold price went the other way, up 1.1%
to $449,900.

**Sellers are holding the line on price, and cutting less.** Price reductions
fell 11.2% to 1,786, and new listings dipped 4.1% to 2,269. The median
new-listing price slipped 4.1% to $429,990, which reads as sellers listing a
touch lower to start, not as a market that is falling.

**Buyers found a little more room.** Homes that closed sold for a median 98.4%
of their original list price, down from 99.6% a week earlier. But measured
against their final list price, the median was still 100.0%, exactly where it
was. That means the extra give came from price cuts made before the sale, not
from buyers pushing accepted prices down. It is the cost of launching too
high. See the Deep Dive below.

**Closings bounced back, but this is a rebound, not a surge.** Closed sales rose
39.0% to 984 after the Labor Day week, yet that is still 14.4% below the
1,150 closings in the week before the holiday. Pending contracts were flat
at 482. Total closed volume was $533.0 million.

**The rate spike has not shown up in the closed data yet, and that is the thing to
watch.** Homes that closed this week were mostly contracts signed weeks ago,
before the rate moved from 6.76% to 6.95%. The next two to three weeks of
pending contracts are where the rate would show up if it is going to.

## The Friedman Signal: Cooling

Maryland's housing market cooled this week, and it did it quietly. Prices
held and even ticked up, price cuts fell, and sellers listed a bit more
conservatively. What changed is the clock: homes took five more days to go
under contract, and buyers took a little more off the original ask. Then
Freddie Mac put the mortgage rate at 6.95%, nearly seven, the fourth
increase in a row. Closings rebounded from the holiday week, but they are
mostly contracts written before the rate moved. Watch the next two or three
weeks of pending contracts and days to contract. That is where a rate
this high starts to show, if it is going to.

## Market Spotlight: The 11 Biggest Markets

Maryland's 11 largest counties by closings this week made up 83% of the
state's sales. Here is how each one moved.

**Montgomery County.** The state's biggest market closed 151 homes at a
$642,215 median, up 2.8% from last week. Homes took longer to sell, a 37-day
average versus 25 (the median was 23, so a few long-sitting listings pulled the
average up), and sold for a median 98.0% of original list. New listings fell
10.2% to 377, and the new-listing-to-closing ratio eased to 2.5x from 4.5x.

**Anne Arundel County.** 110 closings at a $532,500 median, up 6.5%, with a
34-day average (median 13) and homes selling at 98.7% of original list. New
listings dipped 5.6% to 203, a 1.8x ratio that makes it one of the tighter
large markets.

**Prince George's County.** 104 closings at a $471,995 median. That is up from
$414,000, though last week's figure sat on a thin holiday-week sample of 49
closings. The average days on market improved to 33 from 37, and homes sold at
a median 100.0% of original list. New listings held flat at 282, and the
supply ratio dropped to 2.7x from 5.7x as closings returned.

**Baltimore County.** 102 closings at a $381,000 median, up 2.3%, with a 27-day
average (median 12), the third-fastest market in the state, and homes selling
at 99.8% of original list. It also led the state in pending contracts with 88.
New listings rose 10.9% to 275, a 2.7x ratio.

**Baltimore City.** 86 closings at a $225,500 median, down 4.6%, and a
45-day average. It carries the most supply relative to closings among the
large counties, 3.2x, with 276 new listings, down 5.2%. Homes sold at a
median 98.7% of original list.

**Frederick County.** 67 closings at a $527,425 median, up from $455,000.
New listings fell 27.6% to 97, giving Frederick the tightest supply ratio in the
state at 1.4x, and homes sold at 99.6% of original list, near the top of the
large counties. The average days on market rose to 37 from 29, but the median
was 20.

**Howard County.** 51 closings at a $700,000 median, the highest of any
county with a meaningful sample and up from $595,000 last week. A 26-day
average made it the second-fastest market in the state. New listings fell
14.0% to 92, a 1.8x ratio, and homes sold at 98.7% of original list.

**Harford County.** 47 closings at a $425,000 median, up 9.1%, and the pace
snapped back to a 30-day average from 48 last week (median 18). New listings
slipped 6.0% to 94 with 33 pending contracts, a 2.0x ratio. Homes sold at
98.3% of original list.

**Worcester County.** 37 closings at a $464,200 median, down from $525,000,
with a 66-day average (median 44). Worcester had the softest pricing among the
big markets: homes sold for a median 95.8% of original list and 97.4% of
final list. New listings rose 12.5% to 72, a 1.9x ratio. This is the resort
market to watch as the season ends.

**Charles County.** 36 closings at a $425,000 median, down from $479,500,
with the average days on market stretching to 59 from 35 (median 41). New
listings fell 18.4% to 84, a 2.3x ratio, and homes sold at 98.1% of original
list.

**Carroll County (Kyle's home county).** 29 closings at a $360,000 median,
down from $449,000 last week. Medians swing with the mix of homes that close
in a small week, and homes here still sold at a median 100.0% of original
list, so this looks like a mix of smaller homes closing rather than a price
drop. The average was 44 days (median 21). Carroll is also among the tightest
markets in the state: 43 new listings, down 21.8%, a 1.5x ratio.

## One Thing I'd Do This Week

**If I were buying a home in Maryland:** Lock your rate. It rose 19 basis
points this week, which is about $51 more a month on a $400,000 loan, and it
has now risen four weeks running. The upside is that buyers are getting a
little more room: homes sold at 98.4% of original list, and with 2,269 new
listings there is inventory to choose from. Spend that leverage on price and
terms, not on waiting for a better rate.

**If I were selling a home in Maryland:** Price for week one. The typical
home took 25 days to go under contract this week, five more than last week,
and the gap between what sellers first asked and what they got widened to
1.6 points. Homes priced right on day one are still selling at full final
list price. The ones that launch high pay for it in time.

**If I were investing in Maryland real estate:** Look where the clock is
slowest and the pricing is softest. Garrett (102 days) and Caroline (98 days)
are moving slowest, and Worcester sold at 95.8% of original list, which is
where motivated sellers and real negotiating room live right now. On the
supply side, Baltimore City and Wicomico carry the most inventory relative to
closings at 3.2x each.

## Deep Dive: The Number That Explains This Week

**1.6 points**

That is the gap between what sellers first asked and what they finally got
for the typical Maryland home that closed this week: 98.4% of the original
list price, versus 100.0% of the final list price. Last week that gap was 0.4
points. It quadrupled in seven days.

Here is why it matters. Sellers did not lose ground at the closing table. The
median home still sold for exactly its final list price, the same as last
week. What changed is how many sellers had to get there by cutting. The 1.6
points is the price they gave up between launching and settling, and on a
$450,000 home it is about $7,200, compared with about $1,800 last week.

The 1.6 points is only half the cost. The other half is time: 25 days to go
under contract instead of 20. A price cut needs days to work its way through
buyers, and this week the typical wait for a contract got longer. The
cheapest price cut is the one you never have to make.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Apple Cider Pork Chops

Maryland apple season is in full swing, from the orchards in Carroll and
Frederick counties to the farm stands along the Shore. This is a one-skillet
dinner that leans on exactly what is in season, and it comes together in about
35 minutes.

**Serves 4**

- 4 bone-in pork chops, about 1 inch thick
- 1 teaspoon salt and 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, sliced
- 2 firm apples (Honeycrisp or Stayman Winesap), cored and cut into 8 wedges each
- 1 cup unfiltered apple cider
- 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 teaspoon dried)
- 1 tablespoon butter

1. Pat the chops dry and season both sides with the salt and pepper.
2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the chops about
   4 minutes per side until golden, then move them to a plate.
3. Lower the heat to medium. Cook the onion for 4 minutes, then add the apples
   and cook 3 minutes more.
4. Pour in the cider, mustard, vinegar, and thyme, scraping up the browned
   bits, and simmer 3 minutes.
5. Nestle the chops back in, cover, and cook on low 8 to 10 minutes, until the
   center reaches 145 degrees F. Let them rest 3 minutes.
6. Stir the butter into the pan sauce and spoon the apples, onions, and sauce
   over the chops.

Serve with mashed potatoes or roasted sweet potatoes and a bitter green salad
to balance the sweetness.

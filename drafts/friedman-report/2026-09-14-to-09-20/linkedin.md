Denise and Walt listed their Frederick County house on Monday. Their agent said $525,000. Walt wanted $549,900. "We can always come down."

Then Freddie Mac put the 30-year rate at 6.95% on Thursday, up 19 basis points in one week, the fourth increase in a row. That is about $51 more a month on a $400,000 loan than the week before. Every buyer who toured their house was running that math.

Here is what they could not see from their kitchen table, and what this week's Maryland data shows:

→ Median days to contract went from 20 to 25, the biggest weekly slowdown this report has tracked
→ Median sold price still rose 1.1% to $449,900. Price held. Time gave.
→ Buyers paid 98.4% of original list price, down from 99.6%, while the median home still sold for 100.0% of its FINAL list price
→ That 1.6 point gap between first ask and final price is about $7,200 on a $450,000 home, up from about $1,800 last week

The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 44/100, Balanced Market, Cooling. Demand 54, Seller Strength 55, Market Speed 42, Rate Environment 24.

Denise and Walt read the numbers on Friday, dropped to $529,900, and by Sunday night had an offer in hand.

The read: if you're selling, price for week one. The cheapest price cut is the one you never have to make. If you're buying, lock your rate now and spend your leverage on price and terms, not on waiting.

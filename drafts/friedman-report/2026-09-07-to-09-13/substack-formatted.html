Title: Everyone Waited for the Same Week to List | Maryland Weekly Market Report, Week of September 7 – 13, 2026
Subtitle: 2,366 new listings hit Maryland in a single week. Sellers who priced right got more of their money anyway.

Carla Reyes had her Carroll County house ready to list back in July. Her agent talked her out of it.

“Everyone’s telling you the same thing right now,” he said. “Wait for after Labor Day.” So she waited. And on September 8th, her listing went live, right along with 2,365 other Maryland sellers who had the exact same idea.

Statewide new listings jumped 17.4% in a single week, the biggest one-week supply increase this report has tracked, to a median asking price of $448,383, itself up 6.8%. Carla watched the portal that first weekend and felt her stomach drop a little. Hundreds of new listings, all competing for the same buyers, in the same seven days.

Here’s what she didn’t see: that same week, homes that actually made it to the closing table sold for a median 99.6% of their original asking price, up from 99.0% the week before. Closed sales fell hard, 708 versus 1,150, but that’s mostly a Labor Day story: title companies and attorneys don’t love scheduling settlements around the holiday. Pending contracts, the cleaner demand read, only slipped 7.1%.

Her agent priced her house at what the Carroll County comps actually supported. Within four days, against a flood of new competition, she had a signed contract at 98% of ask.

This Week's Price Extremes

Priciest listing: 6920 Travelers Rest Cir, Easton, $9,950,000. A 20.58-acre waterfront estate on the Tred Avon River in Talbot County, 529 feet of water frontage, a private pier with three slips and two boat lifts, a heated pool, and a separate guest house.

Most affordable listing: 10509 Burton Park, Frostburg, $15,000. A manufactured home on a rented lot in Allegany County, sold as-is, lot rent $450 a month. Same state, same week, a spread of nearly $9.94 million.

  • 💰 Median Sold Price: $445,000, up 1.1% from $440,000

Fastest county this week: St. Mary’s County, 14-day average days on market across 13 closings.

Slowest county: Garrett County, 93-day average days on market on 10 closings.

The Friedman Market Momentum Index: 48/100, Balanced Market, Pricing Power Returns

Down two points from last week, but the internals split. Demand cooled on the holiday week (Demand Score 43) and rates rose a third straight week (Rate Environment 32). But sellers actually gained ground: Seller Strength climbed to 56 and Market Speed held at 60. The takeaway: a calendar-driven pause on volume, sitting on top of a market that’s still fundamentally healthy.

Deep Dive: The Number That Explains This Week

99.6% is the median ratio of sold price to original list price across every Maryland home that closed this week, up from 99.0% the week before. It rose in the same week 2,366 new listings, a 17.4% jump, hit the market asking 6.8% more money.

Conventional wisdom says a flood of new competition should cost sellers leverage. It didn’t. That’s a market telling you demand is deep enough to absorb a real supply surge without buyers gaining the upper hand on price, even while they’re gaining more homes to choose from.

One Thing I'd Do This Week

Buying? Use the wave. There are 350 more homes to choose from than last week, and Prince George’s and Montgomery counties are seeing new supply build faster than it’s closing, real room to negotiate. Just lock your rate: 6.76% is the third straight weekly increase.

Selling? You have a lot of company this week, 2,366 other new listings, but that isn’t a reason to underprice. Homes that closed this week held 99.6% of original ask, better than the week before. Price to your comps, not to fear of the crowd.

Investing? Garrett (93-day avg) and Wicomico (67-day avg) remain the slowest-moving counties in the state, easiest place to find a motivated seller. On the supply side, Prince George’s and Montgomery are building inventory fastest relative to what’s closing.

Bonus From My Kitchen: End-of-Garden Pico de Gallo

The last real tomatoes of the season are still coming off Eastern Shore vines, before the first cool snap ends the run. A simple, fresh pico de gallo that lets a countertop of end-of-summer tomatoes speak for itself. Makes about 3 cups.

  1. Dice 4 to 5 ripe tomatoes (seeds and excess liquid removed) and combine with 1/2 cup finely diced white onion, 2 minced jalapenos (seeds removed for less heat), 1/2 cup chopped fresh cilantro, and 2 minced garlic cloves.
  2. Squeeze in the juice of 2 limes and season with salt to taste.
  3. Let it sit at room temperature for 15 to 20 minutes so the flavors come together.

Scoop with tortilla chips or spoon over grilled chicken, fish, or eggs. Best eaten the same day.

## Long Version (~1,400 characters)

Maryland's housing market reset for fall this week.

New listings jumped 17.4% to 2,366, the biggest one-week supply surge
this report has tracked, sellers coming back from Labor Day all at once
and asking a median 6.8% more money doing it. Closings fell 38.4% to
708, mostly a holiday settlement-calendar effect (title companies and
attorneys don't love scheduling around Labor Day), not a demand break.
Pending contracts only slipped 7.1%.

Here's the surprise: sellers who closed this week did better on price,
not worse. The median sold price rose 1.1% to $445,000, and closed homes
realized a median 99.6% of their original list price, up from 99.0% the
week before, even against the heaviest new-listing week on record.

The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 48 out of 100: Balanced
Market, Pricing Power Returns.

New this week: a full county-by-county breakdown of where new supply is
landing fastest. Prince George's, Baltimore City, and Montgomery County
saw new listings build well ahead of closings, real room for buyers to
negotiate there. St. Mary's County was the fastest-moving market in the
state (14-day average, 13 closings).

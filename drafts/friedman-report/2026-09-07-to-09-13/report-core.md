## The Story of the Week

Carla Reyes had her Carroll County house ready to list back in July. Her
agent talked her out of it.

"Everyone's telling you the same thing right now," he said. "Wait for
after Labor Day. Buyers come back to school-year mode, sellers who were
sitting on the fence all list at once, and you don't want to be one more
house nobody's looking at in the middle of August."

So she waited. And on September 8th, her listing went live, right along
with 2,365 other Maryland sellers who had the exact same idea. Statewide
new listings jumped 17% in a single week, the biggest one-week supply
increase this report has tracked. Carla watched the portal that first
weekend and felt her stomach drop a little. Hundreds of new listings, all
competing for the same buyers, in the same seven days.

Here's what she didn't see: that same week, homes that actually made it
to the closing table sold for a median 99.6% of their original asking
price, up from 99.0% the week before. Not down. Up. Fewer homes closed
overall, mostly a holiday-week settlement lull, attorneys and title
companies don't love scheduling closings around Labor Day, but the ones
that did close held their price better than they had in weeks.

Her agent priced her house at what the Carroll County comps actually
supported, not what she hoped a bidding war might produce. Within four
days, against a flood of new competition, she had a signed contract at
98% of ask.

The lesson isn't that more competition doesn't matter. It does. It's that
the market absorbed a historic wave of new supply in a single week
without sellers losing pricing power, which tells you demand is still
there to meet it. If you've been waiting for "after Labor Day" like
everyone else, you are not late. You're on time, along with a lot of
company. Price like it.

## What the Numbers Really Mean

**Closings dropped sharply, and it's mostly the calendar, not the
market.** Closed sales fell 38.4% to 708, the lowest weekly total this
report has tracked. September 7th was Labor Day. Title companies and
attorneys' offices routinely avoid scheduling settlements on or right
around the holiday, which compresses closings into the weeks before and
after it, not necessarily through it. Pending contracts, a cleaner
forward-looking read on demand, fell a much milder 7.1% to 481, which
fits a holiday-week pause better than a genuine demand break.

**New listings surged the hardest this report has measured.** 2,366
homes came onto the market this week, up 17.4% from 2,016, at a median
asking price of $448,383, itself up 6.8%. This is the "wait for after
Labor Day" seller instinct playing out statewide, all at once. It's also
why price reductions actually fell 4.1% to 2,011: a wave of brand-new,
freshly priced listings dilutes the pool of stale, previously-reduced
ones.

**And sellers who closed this week did better, not worse.** The median
sold-to-original-list ratio rose to 99.6% from 99.0%, and median days to
contract ticked down a day, to 20. Even flooded with new competition, the
homes that transacted held their price. That combination, a supply surge
sellers can price the market's confidence in fall demand, not a market
running scared.

## The Friedman Signal™: Resetting

Maryland's housing market reset for fall this week. A holiday week
knocked closings down hard and cooled pending contracts a little, but
that's a calendar story more than a demand story. The real signal is on
the supply side: sellers came back from Labor Day in force, 2,366 new
listings, the biggest weekly jump this report has tracked, and asked for
more money doing it, a 6.8% jump in median new-listing price. And the

## Bonus From My Kitchen: End-of-Garden Pico de Gallo

The last real tomatoes of the season are still coming off Eastern Shore
vines right now, before the first cool snap ends the run. This is the
week to use them: a simple, fresh pico de gallo that lets a countertop
full of end-of-summer tomatoes speak for itself.

**Makes about 3 cups**

- 4 to 5 ripe tomatoes, diced, seeds and excess liquid removed
- 1/2 cup white onion, finely diced
- 2 jalapenos, minced (seeds removed for less heat)
- 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- Juice of 2 limes
- Salt, to taste

1. Combine the tomatoes, onion, jalapenos, cilantro, and garlic in a
   bowl.
2. Squeeze in the lime juice and season with salt to taste.
3. Let it sit at room temperature for 15 to 20 minutes so the flavors
   come together before serving.

Scoop with tortilla chips or spoon over grilled chicken, fish, or eggs.
Best eaten the same day, while the tomatoes are still garden fresh.

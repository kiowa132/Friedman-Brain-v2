# Video Script: The Friedman Report, Week of September 7-13, 2026

Runtime target: 2.5 to 3.5 minutes at a normal speaking pace. Read straight
through, pause where marked. Written to be read aloud, not literary.

---

Hey everyone, Kyle Friedman here with The Friedman Team, and this is your
Friedman Report for the week of September 7th through the 13th.

This was a big one. Maryland just had its biggest new-listing week on
record. 2,366 new listings hit the market, that's up 17.4% in a single
week, the largest one-week jump I've tracked since I started doing this.

[pause]

Here's why. A lot of sellers deliberately wait until after Labor Day to
list. Buyers come back into a routine, the summer travel slows down, and
sellers figure they'll get more eyes on the house. Well, everybody had
that same idea at the exact same time this year, and it showed up in the
data in a big way.

Now, closings told a different story. Closed sales fell 38.4%, down to
708 for the week. Before you panic, that's mostly the calendar. September
7th was Labor Day, and title companies and attorneys just don't love
scheduling settlements around a holiday. Pending contracts, which is the
better read on real demand, only slipped 7.1%. So this isn't buyers
disappearing, it's a scheduling gap.

[pause]

And here's the number that actually surprised me this week. Even with
all that new competition flooding the market, homes that closed sold for
99.6% of their original asking price. That's up from 99.0% the week
before. Sellers didn't lose leverage, they gained it. The market
absorbed the biggest supply wave I've tracked without buyers getting the
upper hand on price.

The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 48 out of 100 this week,
Balanced Market, Pricing Power Returns. Demand cooled a little because
of the holiday, but seller strength actually climbed.

[pause]

County by county, St. Mary's County was the fastest-moving market in the
state, a 14-day average days on market. On the other end, Garrett
County's the slowest, 93 days on average. And if you're a buyer, keep an
eye on Prince George's and Montgomery counties, new listings there are
building a lot faster than they're closing, which means more room to
negotiate.

We also had the two most extreme listings I've seen side by side in one
week. A $9,950,000 waterfront estate on the Tred Avon River in Talbot
County, and a $15,000 manufactured home in Frostburg. Same state, same
seven days, almost a $10 million spread.

[pause]

So here's my read. If you're buying, use the wave, there's real
inventory and real negotiating room right now, especially in Prince
George's and Montgomery. Just get your rate locked, because it's gone up
three weeks in a row. If you're selling, don't panic about the
competition. Price to your comps and the data says you'll still get
close to full ask.

If you want to know what this means for your specific street or your
specific situation, reach out, my number and info are right on the
screen. I'm Kyle Friedman with The Friedman Team, and I'll see you next
week.

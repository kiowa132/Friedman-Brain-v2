## The Story of the Week

Renee and Theo Alvarez started looking in April.

By mid-July they had been outbid three times, once by $40,000 over asking
on a house they had walked through for eleven minutes. They decided to
sit the rest of the summer out and come back when things calmed down.

They came back the week after Labor Day, and there was a little more room
than there had been in the spring. There were 2,016 new listings across
Maryland that week, and homes were taking a median of 21 days to go under
contract, up from 18 the week before. And 2,098 listings had cut their
asking price.

The house they liked was in Frederick County, where the median sale
landed at about $484,000 this week. It had been on the market a little
over three weeks and the sellers had already trimmed the price once. Theo
wanted to come in low. But their lender's quote had just moved from
6.66% to 6.71%, and on a 30-year note every eighth of a point is real
money. So instead of a lowball they offered near the reduced list price,
asked the sellers to cover part of the closing costs, and locked their
rate that same afternoon.

It was accepted in two days. Not because they won a bidding war, but
because on that house, this week, there wasn't one to win.

Here is what I would tell Renee and Theo, and anyone else reading this
week's numbers. Maryland is basically holding still: about the same
volume of homes selling, about the same volume coming on, with a small
give-back on price and a few days added to the typical time-to-contract
as fall settles in. Not a boom. Not a stall. Steady, softening slightly
at the edges. For a buyer with a rate lock in hand, that is a workable
week. For a seller, it means the list price has to be right, because the
market is no longer in a hurry to forgive an ambitious one.

## What the Numbers Really Mean

**Volume held steady; prices softened at the edges.** Closings edged up
1.1% to 1,150. New listings were essentially flat at 2,016. Pending
contracts rose 5.7% to 518, and price reductions barely moved at 2,098.
Those are ordinary weekly wiggles, the kind you see in a market that is
neither accelerating nor breaking down.

**The real movement this week was a mild softening on price and pace.**
The median sold price slipped 2.2% to $440,000, and the median
new-listing price fell the same $10,000, to $419,900. The median
time-to-contract slowed from 18 days to 21. And the mortgage rate rose
for a second straight week. None of these is a large move on its own, but
they all point the same way: a little more room for buyers, a little less
urgency behind sellers, right on schedule for the turn into fall.

**The softening is real but shallow.** Even with 2,098 price reductions
statewide and a $10,000 dip in the median, the typical home that closed
this week still sold for 99.0% of its *original* list price. Sellers are
trimming, but they are not capitulating.

## Market Spotlight: Carroll County & Harford County

**Carroll County**
Carroll County closed 44 homes this week at a $533,000 median, and here
is the number that stands out: closed homes sold at an average of 101.0%
of their *original* list price. Not their reduced price, their first one.
In a week when the statewide median sold price fell and price cuts stayed
elevated, Carroll sellers were still beating their opening ask. The
average days on market reads 30, but the median is 10, so the typical
Carroll home is going under contract fast and a handful of longer-sitting
listings are pulling the average up. Kyle's home county remains one of
the tightest in the state.

## One Thing I'd Do This Week

**If I were buying a home in Maryland:** Get the rate lock first, then
shop. The rate moved against buyers again this week and it is trending
up, but the extra inventory and the 2,098 price cuts mean you can push on
price and terms in a way you could not in the spring. Spend your leverage
on the contract, not on waiting for a better week.

**If I were selling a home in Maryland:** Price to your first number, not
your second. The homes that needed a cut this week are the ones still
sitting. The ones priced right on day one are still going under contract
in about three weeks at essentially full ask (99.0% of original list
price, median, statewide).

**If I were investing in Maryland real estate:** Look where days on
market is stretching, Talbot (128), Garrett (114), Kent (76), Dorchester
(74). Thinner buyer pools and longer holds, but that is also where the
motivated sellers and the real negotiating room are right now.

## Deep Dive: The Surprising Fact This Week

**99.0%**

That is the median ratio of sold price to *original* list price across
every Maryland home that closed this week. Not the most recent list
price, the very first one.

It matters because of everything happening around it. There were 2,098
price reductions statewide this week. The median sold price fell $10,000.
Homes are taking longer to go under contract. By every one of those
measures, this looks like a market where sellers are losing ground. And
yet the typical closed sale still landed within one percent of the number
the seller asked on day one.

The reconciliation: the price weakness this week is concentrated in the
listings that mispriced at launch and had to chase the market down. The
homes that were priced correctly from the start are still selling at, or
very near, full ask. In a softening market, the gap between those two
outcomes widens, and this week it was wide.

*(The average sold-to-original ratio was 97.6%, pulled below the median
by a smaller number of deeply discounted sales.)*

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Maryland Crab & Corn Chowder

The first cool nights of September, the last of the local sweet corn, and
crab still running in the Bay. There is exactly one week a year this dish
is perfectly in season, and this is it.

**Serves 4–6**
- 4 strips bacon, chopped
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 4 cups seafood or chicken stock
- 2 medium Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and diced
- 3 ears fresh corn, kernels cut off (about 2 cups; frozen works off-season)
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 1 lb Maryland lump crab meat, picked over for shell
- 1 tsp Old Bay, plus more to taste
- Salt and black pepper
- Chopped chives or parsley, to finish

1. In a heavy pot, cook the bacon over medium heat until crisp. Remove
   with a slotted spoon and set aside, leaving the fat in the pot.
2. Add the onion and celery to the bacon fat and cook until soft, about
   5 minutes. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute more.
3. Whisk in the stock a little at a time so it stays smooth. Add the
   potatoes, bring to a simmer, and cook until the potatoes are tender,
   about 12 minutes.
4. Add the corn and cream and simmer 5 minutes.
5. Gently fold in the crab and Old Bay and warm through, about 2
   minutes. Do not let it boil hard or the crab toughens.
6. Season with salt, pepper, and more Old Bay to taste. Serve topped with
   the crispy bacon and chives.

Make it a full dinner with crusty bread and a green salad. It reheats
well, but add the crab fresh to each reheated bowl if you can.

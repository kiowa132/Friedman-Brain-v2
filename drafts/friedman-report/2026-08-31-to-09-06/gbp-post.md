## Long Version (~1,400 characters)

Maryland's housing market is holding steady.

A quick correction first: last week's report showed closings and new
listings "nearly doubling." That was a data-coverage issue on our end,
not a real market move. Two weeks ago the Bright MLS pull was missing
several county feeds, so those totals were undercounted. This week is the
first genuine full-state week-over-week comparison in a month.

And it shows a market close to flat. Closings held at 1,150, new listings
were flat at 2,016, and pending contracts rose 5.7% to 518. What actually
moved was price and pace: median sold price fell 2.2% to $440,000, median
days-to-contract slowed from 18 to 21, and Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed
rose 5 basis points to 6.71%, its second straight weekly increase. A mild
autumn softening, not a turn.

The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 50 out of 100 — Balanced Market,
Holding Steady. Nominally down from 58, but most of that gap is last
week's inflated number correcting itself.

Even with 2,098 price reductions statewide, the typical closed home still
sold for 99.0% of its original list price — the softening is real but
shallow, and it's concentrated in listings that mispriced at launch.
Harford County was the fastest market in the state; Carroll County
sellers still sold above their original list price.

## Long Version (~1,400 characters)

Maryland's housing market leveled off this week.

Last week's surge didn't reverse, it just stopped accelerating. Closings
held at 1,150 (up 1.1%), new listings were flat at 2,016, and pending
contracts rose 5.7% to 518. Same elevated volume, no further climb. But
the price and pace numbers all ticked the other way. Median sold price
fell 2.2% to $440,000, giving back last week's gain. Median
days-to-contract slowed from 18 to 21. And Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed
rose 5 basis points to 6.71%, its second straight weekly increase.

The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 52 out of 100 this week —
Balanced Market, Momentum Cooling — down from 58. It's the first FMMI
decline in three weeks, and a broad one: Demand eased to 68, Seller
Strength to 46, Market Speed to 60, Rate Environment to 34.

Harford County posted the fastest pace in Maryland (25-day average
across 55 closings, homes selling at full list). Carroll County closed
homes sold at an average 101% of their original list price, a $533,000
median, still beating opening ask. And for the second week running,
price reductions (2,098) outnumbered new listings (2,016).

The read: a market leveling off, not turning. For a buyer with a rate
lock in hand, it's the friendliest week since spring. For a seller, it's
the week the market started grading your list price more strictly.

Maryland's housing market this week, in one word: leveling.

Last week's surge didn't reverse. It just stopped accelerating. Closings
held at 1,150 (up 1.1%), new listings were flat at 2,016, and pending
contracts rose 5.7% to 518 — the same elevated volume, no further
climb. But the price and pace metrics all ticked the other way: median
sold price fell 2.2% to $440,000, median days-to-contract slowed from 18
to 21, and Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed rose 5 bp to 6.71%, its second
straight weekly increase.

The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 52/100 this week — Balanced
Market, Momentum Cooling — down from 58. It's the first FMMI decline in
three weeks, and a broad one: all four components softened. Demand eased
to 68 (from 78), Seller Strength to 46 (from 50), Market Speed to 60
(from 66), Rate Environment to 34 (from 39).

→ Harford County: fastest market in Maryland — 25-day average across 55
closings, homes selling at full list
→ Carroll County: closed homes sold at an average 101% of ORIGINAL list
price, $533,000 median — still beating opening ask in a week the state
softened
→ Deep dive: for the second week running, price reductions (2,098)
outnumbered new listings (2,016)

The read: this is a market leveling off, not turning. For a buyer with a
rate lock in hand, it's the friendliest week since spring. For a seller,
it's the week the market started grading your list price a lot more
strictly.

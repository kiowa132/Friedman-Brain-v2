![Maryland suburban street with multiple new "For Sale" signs at golden hour](https://cdn.gamma.app/qek416wyv0dua4l/design-anything/ws9qbEeKTuQS3j28CIk95/r9fCzz9IjjfCSr63E4iet.jpg)

Danielle had been told to wait until after Labor Day.

She's a seller near Rockville, in Montgomery County, sitting on a
townhome she'd been debating listing since June. Every conversation with
her agent all summer landed on the same advice: hold off. Let the
late-summer lull pass. Nobody's out looking with the kids still home and
everyone squeezing in one more vacation before school starts. Wait for
the "real" fall market to show up in September, when buyers come back
serious.

Then last week's edition of this report crossed her feed — the one
flagging that new listings had dropped 14% and price cuts had eased right
alongside them, the market quietly tightening in a week most people
assumed was still loosening. She read it twice, decided that was her
signal, and listed Tuesday morning.

She was not alone. Not even close. By Sunday night, roughly 2,019 other
Maryland sellers had made almost the exact same call in the same seven
days. That is not a typo, and it is not a rounding error — new listings
statewide nearly doubled week over week, and I mean that literally: up
89.8% from 1,064 the week before. I have been writing this report long
enough now to say with confidence that is the sharpest single-week supply
move it has ever tracked, in either direction. Nothing else this summer
comes close.

**Here's the full statewide picture for the week of August 24–30:**
- New listings: 2,019, up 89.8% from 1,064
- Closed sales: 1,137, up 89.2% from 601 — closings nearly doubled right
  alongside the new supply, which is the part that actually surprised me
- Pending contracts: 490, up 31.0% from 374
- Price reductions: 2,090, up 80.5% from 1,158
- Median sold price: $450,000, up 2.3% from $440,000
- Homes that closed this week sold at a median 99.3% of their *original*
  asking price — not their most recent list price, their original one
- Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed: 6.66%, up one basis point, ending a
  two-week streak of declines

Danielle watched her showing requests trickle in that first week and
genuinely worried her listing would disappear into the noise of a
thousand others hitting the market at once. It didn't. Priced honestly to
the comps rather than to what she wished the house was worth, it drew
three offers within days and went under contract fast — right in line
with the statewide pace this week, where new listings are moving to
contract in a median of just four days.

![Whiteboard showing this week's Maryland market snapshot: FMMI 58, homes sold 1,137, new listings 2,019, pending 490, median price $450K, price cuts 2,090, 30-year rate 6.66%](https://cdn.gamma.app/qek416wyv0dua4l/design-anything/507Q214cLyqzCDMHfaeo1/Y3Lyx4Ut_b91jmlnfqJF6.jpg)

Here's how I'm reading it, and what I told Danielle when she called me
worried about the wave of competition landing on top of her: a market
that gains this much volume in a single week isn't tilting decisively
toward buyers or sellers. It's recalibrating. A lot of sellers who'd been
sitting on the fence all summer, waiting for some signal that it was
"time," apparently all got the same signal at once and moved. That is
genuinely good news if you're a buyer who's been starved for options
this summer — there is meaningfully more out there to look at this week
than there was seven days ago. But it is not a green light to lowball.
Competition for the *good* listings, the ones priced right in the right
neighborhoods, went up, not down. Danielle's three offers in a matter of
days prove that.

**The Friedman Market Momentum Index landed at 58 out of 100 this week —
Balanced Market, Surging Activity** — up modestly from 55 last week. I
want to spend a minute on why the headline number only moved +3 despite
some of the largest single-week swings this report has ever recorded,
because it's the most interesting part of this week's data. The FMMI is
built from four components, and this week they genuinely pulled in
opposite directions instead of moving together the way they usually do:

- **Demand Score jumped to 78** (from 62) — closings surged 89.2% and
  pending contracts rose 31.0% in the same seven days. That's by far the
  strongest demand signal I've tracked since starting this index.
- **Seller Strength Score actually fell to 50** (from 60) — median price
  gains slowed to 2.3% (from 4.8% the week before), while price
  reductions surged 80.5%. Some of that is just proportional to how many
  more listings there suddenly were, not sellers panicking, but it's a
  real step back from where seller leverage sat two weeks running.
- **Market Speed Score improved to 66** (from 55) — days to contract
  dropped sharply this week, consistent with a market absorbing volume
  quickly rather than choking on it.
- **Rate Environment Score softened to 39** (from 42) — the two-week
  streak of rate declines ended with a token one-basis-point uptick to
  6.66%, not a meaningful move but enough to nudge this component down.

Average those four together and you get 58 — a market busier on every
front at once, not one clearly favoring either side.

**This week's price extremes were a genuine study in contrasts**, and I
mean that more literally than usual. The priciest listing on the market
this week was **6210 Dahlonega Rd in Bethesda** — a brand-new, 2026-built
modern estate in the Glen Echo Heights neighborhood, eight bedrooms,
seven full and one half baths, nearly 6,900 total square feet between the
main levels and a full daylight walkout lower level, built by Edgewood
Builders and listed at **$3,995,000**. It's the kind of listing — a
second kitchen, three fireplaces, an 11,375 square foot lot minutes from
the C&O Canal and the Capital Crescent Trail — that only shows up when a
market this active starts pulling out its best inventory, not just its
most plentiful. On the completely opposite end, **2545 W Lafayette Ave in
Baltimore City** hit the market this week as an online auction listing in
shell condition, needing a complete renovation, with bidding opening at
just **$10,000**. Same state, same seven-day window, a spread of nearly
four million dollars. That range is worth sitting with for a second — the
statewide median of $450,000 is sitting on top of an enormous amount of
variation underneath it.

**Where I'd focus if you're buying or selling right now:** Carroll County
holds the fastest pace in the state for a second straight week running —
19 days average, down from 23 last week, and it got *faster* while
simultaneously adding volume, 38 closings versus 36 the week before.
That's a real signal, not a small-sample fluke. If you're sitting on a
farm or equestrian property out there, this remains a market rewarding
honest pricing without a fight. Montgomery County — Danielle's county —
posted the highest closing volume of any county in the state this week,
168 homes, still moving at a healthy 28-day pace. That's proof the
state's biggest, priciest markets can absorb real volume without losing
speed, which isn't something you can always count on.

One county worth flagging on the slow end: Dorchester, which was already
last week's slowest county at 66 days and got even slower this week, 85
days. It's still sitting on a thin sample of just five closings both
weeks running, so I'm not calling it a trend yet, but two weeks at the
bottom of the pace rankings is starting to be a pattern worth watching
rather than dismissing as noise.

**The fun-fact number of the week:** add up every home that closed in
Maryland this week and you get $606,704,645 in total sales volume across
1,137 transactions — nearly double last week's closed-volume figure,
tracking right alongside the closings count itself. Meanwhile, 432
listings statewide (220 withdrawn, 212 canceled) fell out of the pipeline
this week without ever selling. For every 10 homes that successfully
closed, roughly four others quietly didn't make it. The market's most
visible number is always the one that sells; the ones that don't rarely
make headlines, and this week there were a lot of them.

One thing I'd do this week, depending on which side of the table you're
on: if you're buying, go back to your search — there is meaningfully more
to choose from than there was a week ago, but don't assume that means
easy negotiating room, since well-priced homes are still closing near
full ask. If you're selling, know that you're not listing into an empty
market anymore; almost twice as many sellers listed alongside you, so
pricing to the comps at launch matters more now than it did two weeks ago
when scarce inventory could forgive an ambitious number. And if you're
investing, keep an eye on whether next week's new-listing count holds
anywhere near 2,000 or snaps back toward the 1,000-1,200 range we saw all
summer — a second week at this level would mean fall genuinely arrived
early this year.

Last thing before I go, because it's Labor Day weekend and I can't help
myself: we smoked a block of cream cheese two ways this week for a
cookout, and both versions disappeared embarrassingly fast. One savory —
dry ranch seasoning and garlic powder, finished with chopped chives — and
one sweet-and-spicy, brown sugar and smoked paprika with a pinch of
cayenne, drizzled with honey off the smoker. Score a cold block, coat it
in olive oil so the seasoning sticks, smoke at 225°F for about two hours,
and serve warm with crackers. It's absurdly low-effort for how good it
is — possibly the only easy thing about this market right now. Full

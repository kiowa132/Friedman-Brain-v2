TITLE: Two Thousand Sellers Had the Same Idea This Week | The Friedman Report | Week of August 24–30, 2026

SUBTITLE: Danielle waited all summer for the "right moment" to list near Rockville. So did everyone else.

BYLINE: Kyle Friedman — Aug 31, 2026

---

Danielle had been told to wait until after Labor Day.

She's a seller near Rockville, in Montgomery County, sitting on a
townhome she'd been debating listing since June. Every conversation with
her agent all summer landed on the same advice: hold off, let the
late-summer lull pass, wait for the "real" fall market to show up in
September.

Then last week's edition of this report crossed her feed, the one
flagging that new listings had dropped 14% and price cuts had eased right
alongside them. She read it, decided that was her signal, and listed
Tuesday morning.

She was not alone. Not even close.

By Sunday night, roughly 2,019 other Maryland sellers had made almost the
exact same call in the same seven days. New listings statewide nearly
doubled week over week — up 89.8% from 1,064 the week before, the
sharpest single-week supply move this report has ever tracked, in either
direction. Closings nearly doubled right alongside them, up 89.2% to
1,137, which is the part that actually surprised me.

Median sold price climbed to $450,000, up 2.3% from $440,000. Homes that
closed this week sold at a median 99.3% of their *original* asking price
— not their most recent list price, their original one. And mortgage
rates barely moved: Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed ticked up one basis point
to 6.66%, ending a two-week streak of declines.

I called Danielle to tell her what the numbers were actually saying: a
market that gains this much volume in a single week isn't tilting toward
buyers or sellers, it's recalibrating. A lot of sellers who'd been sitting
on the fence all summer apparently got the same signal at once and moved.
That's real news for buyers who've been starved for options. But it also
means competition for the *good* listings went up, not down — priced
right, Danielle's home drew three offers and went under contract within
the week anyway.

## The Friedman Market Momentum Index: 58/100 — Balanced Market, Surging Activity

Up modestly from 55 last week, but the headline number undersells how
much moved underneath it. Demand Score jumped to 78 (from 62) on the
closings-and-pending surge, while Seller Strength Score actually fell to
50 (from 60) as price gains slowed and reductions spiked. Market Speed
improved to 66; Rate Environment softened to 39 as the rate decline
streak ended. Four components pulling in different directions is why this
week's headline number moved only +3 despite historic swings underneath.

## Deep Dive: The Number That Explains This Week

$606,704,645 — total closed sales volume across 1,137 transactions
statewide this week, nearly double last week's figure, tracking right
alongside the closings count itself.

Here's the flip side: 432 listings statewide (220 withdrawn, 212
canceled) fell out of the pipeline this week without ever selling. For
every 10 homes that closed, roughly 4 others quietly didn't make it. The
market's most visible number is always the one that sells; the ones that
don't rarely make headlines, and this week there were a lot of them.

## One Thing I'd Do This Week

**Buying?** Go back to your search. New listings nearly doubled — there's
meaningfully more to choose from than a week ago. Don't assume that means
easy negotiating room, though: well-priced homes are still closing near
full ask.

**Selling?** You're not listing into an empty market anymore — almost
twice as many sellers listed alongside you. Pricing to the comps at
launch matters more now than it did two weeks ago, when scarce inventory
could forgive an ambitious number.

**Investing?** Watch whether next week's new-listing count holds anywhere
near 2,000 or snaps back toward the 1,000-1,200 range we saw most of the
summer. A second week at this level means fall genuinely arrived early
this year.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Smoked Cream Cheese, Two Ways

Labor Day weekend means one more cookout, and this is the easiest thing
I've put on the smoker all summer. We did two flavors — one savory, one
sweet and spicy — and both blocks were gone before the second round of
guests showed up.

**The method** (works for either flavor, serves 6-8 as an appetizer)
1. Preheat your smoker to 225°F. Any wood works — applewood for the ranch
   & garlic, cherry for the brown sugar & paprika.
2. Score the top of a cold 8 oz block of cream cheese in a crosshatch
   pattern, then coat it all over with a little olive oil so the seasoning
   sticks.
3. Coat generously with your topping of choice (below), pressing gently
   so it adheres.
4. Smoke for about 2 hours, until it's warmed through, slightly golden on
   top, and soft enough to spread.
5. Rest 5-10 minutes, then serve warm with crackers or toasted baguette.

**Ranch & Garlic** — 1 packet (or 2 tbsp) dry ranch seasoning + 1 tsp
garlic powder, topped with chopped chives after smoking.

**Brown Sugar & Paprika** — 3 tbsp brown sugar + 1 tsp smoked paprika +
a pinch of cayenne if you want heat, drizzled with honey right off the
smoker.

Zero skill required, disappears in minutes — possibly the only easy thing
about this market right now.

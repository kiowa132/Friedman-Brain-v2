Marcus finally got a rate lock in early August and figured the hard part
was over.

He's the same first-time buyer in Harford County from a couple editions
back — the one who spent the summer waiting for more price cuts and more
room to negotiate. Two weeks ago, this report told him that window was
narrowing: new listings had dropped 14%, and price cuts had dropped right
alongside them. He believed it, and he started writing offers again.

This week, the Maryland housing market did something neither of us
expected: it flooded back open, all at once.

> New listings and closings both nearly doubled in the same week — the
> sharpest simultaneous supply-and-demand jump this report has tracked.

Statewide, new listings jumped 89.8% to 2,019, and closings jumped 89.2%
to 1,137 — both the largest single-week moves this report has tracked, in
either direction. Price reductions surged too, up 80.5% to 2,090. For a
buyer like Marcus, this isn't the slow grind back toward leverage he was
braced for — it's a sudden flood of choice landing all at once, right at
the seasonal turn into fall.

Median sold price kept climbing, up 2.3% to $450,000, and homes that
closed this week sold at a median 99.3% of their *original* asking price —
proof that even with this much new supply hitting at once, well-priced
listings are still closing near full ask. Mortgage rates, meanwhile,
barely moved: Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed ticked up one basis point to
6.66%, ending a two-week streak of declines.

## What This Week's Numbers Really Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

**The market flooded back open, all at once.** New listings jumped 89.8%
and closings jumped 89.2% in the same week — both the sharpest weekly
moves this report has tracked, in either direction. That kind of
simultaneous supply-and-demand surge, right at the turn into fall, reads
less like a shift toward buyers or sellers and more like pent-up decisions
on both sides landing in the same seven days.

**Pricing power held, but reductions surged too.** Median sold price rose
again to $450,000 (+2.3%), and closed homes realized a median 99.3% of
their original list price. But price reductions jumped 80.5% — the flip
side of this much new inventory hitting at once, and partly proportional
to the volume surge rather than a sign sellers are suddenly overreaching.

## County Spotlight: Montgomery County and Carroll County

### Montgomery County Real Estate Market

Montgomery County posted the highest closing volume of any Maryland county
this week — **168 homes closed** at a **28-day average pace**, the
busiest county in the state by transaction count, at a $619,500 median
closed price.

- Active Listings: 259
- Homes Closed: 168
- Average Days on Market: 28 days

### Carroll County Real Estate Market

Carroll County holds onto the top spot for pace in Maryland for a second
straight week, and got faster while adding volume — **19-day average days
on market**, up from 23 days last week, with **38 closings**. It also
leads our core coverage area on pure pricing power: closed homes sold at a
median 100.0% of their original list price.

- Active Listings: 58
- Homes Closed: 38
- Average Days on Market: 19 days

**Bottom line:** Montgomery shows that Maryland's biggest markets can carry
real volume without losing pace, while Carroll proves its multi-week speed
streak wasn't a fluke — it got faster even as the rest of the state
flooded with new inventory.

## The Friedman Market Momentum Index (FMMI)

<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;color:#0F5C63;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:0.08em;font-size:13px;">58 / 100 — Balanced Market, Surging Activity (up from 55 last week)</p>

**What this means:** Maryland's housing market didn't tip toward buyers or
sellers this week — it just got a lot busier, all at once. Nearly every
volume metric roughly doubled, which looks dramatic in the headline
numbers but is genuinely ambiguous underneath: more competition for buyers
*and* more choice, more leverage for sellers *and* more of them cutting
price. That's why the FMMI moved only modestly (+3) despite historically
large swings in the underlying data.

- **Demand Score: 78%** (up from 62) — Closings surged 89.2% and pending
  contracts rose 31.0% in the same week — by far the strongest demand
  signal this report has tracked.
- **Seller Strength Score: 50%** (down from 60) — Median price gains
  slowed to +2.3% (from +4.8%), while price reductions surged 80.5%.
- **Market Speed Score: 66%** (up from 55) — Days-to-contract dropped
  sharply on this vault's Closed-CDOM basis; see the methodology note above.
- **Rate Environment Score: 39%** (down from 42) — The 30-year fixed
  ticked up 1 basis point to 6.66%, ending a two-week decline streak.

## The Friedman Signal™: Surging

This week isn't a story about which side of the market is winning — it's
a story about volume. New listings, closings, pending contracts, and price
reductions all jumped by the largest single-week margins this report has
tracked, several of them nearly doubling. That's consistent with a
seasonal turn — sellers who'd been sitting on listings through late summer
moved at once, and buyers who'd been waiting responded in kind. Watch next
week closely: if volume holds anywhere near this level, late August marks
the real start of Maryland's fall market.

## One Thing I'd Do This Week

**If I were buying a home in Maryland:** This is the week to go back to
your search. New listings nearly doubled statewide — there is meaningfully
more to choose from than there was seven days ago. Don't assume that means

almost twice as many sellers listed alongside you. Pricing to the comps at
launch matters more now than it did two weeks ago, when inventory was
scarce enough to forgive an ambitious number.

**If I were investing in Maryland real estate:** Watch whether next
week's new-listing number holds anywhere near 2,000, or snaps back toward

would mean the fall market genuinely arrived early this year.

## Deep Dive: The Surprising Fact About Maryland Housing This Week

Add up every home that closed in Maryland this week and you get
**$606,704,645** in total sales volume across 1,137 transactions — nearly
double last week's closed volume, in step with the closings count itself.
Meanwhile, **432 listings** statewide were withdrawn or canceled this week
without ever selling (220 withdrawn, 212 canceled) — meaning for every 10
homes that successfully closed, roughly 4 others quietly fell out of the
pipeline entirely.
